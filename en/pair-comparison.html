PAIRED COMPARISON / SAME RERANKER

What does the extra memory for Qwen 4B buy?

Qwen 4B and BGE-M3 use the same Qwen 0.6B reranker on 2,556 paired queries. The results show modest gains in English retrieval and candidate recall, not a universal case for upgrading.

Three decimals reveal a small difference

Five-task mean nDCG is 0.897334 versus 0.895650, displayed as 0.897 and 0.896; both previously rounded to 0.90. Mean Top-1 is slightly higher for BGE: 81.82% versus 81.79%. Higher nDCG does not guarantee a more accurate first result.

Where to consider it: English retrieval and fewer candidate misses

Across 1,148 English-to-English queries, Qwen achieves 89.81% Top-1 versus BGE’s 89.37%: seven Qwen-only hits and two BGE-only hits, a net gain of five. English also has the largest task-level nDCG gain, 0.004410. Chinese-to-English and Chinese-to-Chinese nDCG are slightly higher, but first-result hits decrease by one and two queries, respectively.

With the same reranker, the main input difference is the embedder’s candidate list. Each MLQA query has one gold-relevant document, so Recall@50 also measures whether it enters the pool. Qwen retrieves a net three, four and eight additional gold documents in the three directions. In English, 11 are Qwen-only candidate hits and three are BGE-only. This gives reranking more opportunities, without guaranteeing first place.

When is the additional capacity worth considering?

Combined Q8_0 weights are 4.581 versus 1.186 GiB: 3.395 GiB more, or about 3.86 times as much. These are not measured RAM requirements; indexes and buffers add overhead. Consider Qwen 4B if English retrieval matters, missing a few critical documents is costly, and capacity is available, then validate on your queries. For Chinese first-result hits, limited memory, or corpora resembling these MIRACL subsets, the evidence favors starting with BGE-M3 + Qwen 0.6B.

MIRACL Chinese per-query nDCG is identical, and English differs on only one query. Both achieve 100% candidate Recall@50; this bounded corpus cannot readily show the value of a larger embedder. Corrected ACORD reranking is pending, so old legal scores cannot justify upgrading. No validated breakdown by specialized domain, query length or multi-hop reasoning was performed, so no advantage on those dimensions is claimed.

Local five-task mean query time is 1.116 s for Qwen and 1.166 s for BGE. Separate batches and potentially different candidate lengths prevent treating the roughly 50 ms gap as a general speed advantage for the larger model.

Per-query differences and uncertainty

Of 2,556 paired queries, 2,504 have equal nDCG; Qwen is better on 27 and BGE on 25. We use 10,000 paired source-passage-cluster bootstrap resamples, seed 20261027. All three MLQA nDCG difference intervals cross zero, so a reliable or significant win is not established. The unadjusted English candidate Recall@50 interval is above zero, but multiple metrics are compared; this remains exploratory.
