Understand the average and its limits.

The latest results come from two official batches. Here are the details needed to inspect and reproduce them.

How is the homepage average calculated?

First average over queries within each condition, then average the six condition scores: three MLQA directions, ACORD, and two MIRACL languages. Each condition weighs 16.67%; by dataset family this is 50% MLQA, 33.33% MIRACL and 16.67% ACORD, not equal family weights. Only the three Qwen 4B embedding setups tested in all six are compared. Missing runs are not zero-filled or ranked with different denominators. The MLQA average weights its three directions equally and includes six setups.

Top-1 and nDCG answer different questions

Top-1 is the fraction whose first document meets this study’s relevance threshold. MRR@10 measures the position of the first relevant result; nDCG@10 evaluates the top-ten order. MLQA/MIRACL use binary labels. ACORD retains grades 0–4 with nDCG gain 2^grade−1, while Top-1 uses grade≥1. Unjudged documents receive no relevance gain in the calculation, but are not human-confirmed negatives. Macro nDCG mixes labeling schemes and is descriptive, not a cross-domain accuracy rate.

What was included?

Batch 1 uses all three MLQA validation directions (504, 504 and 1,148 queries) and 57 ACORD test queries: six setups, 13,278 measurements. Batch 2 uses 200 queries per MIRACL dev language: three setups, 1,200 measurements. The 2,613 count is unique within datasets, not independent questions after cross-language deduplication. MLQA includes the previous 100 queries per direction, so this is not an untouched holdout. MIRACL uses the union of all dev judged passages (3,786 Chinese, 7,921 English), not the full Wikipedia corpus. Its 100% Recall@50 only applies to this subset. QASPER, pilots, failed and interrupted attempts are excluded.

How should intervals be read?

Paired bootstrap uses 2,000 replicates and seed 20261023: source-passage clusters for MLQA, parent queries for ACORD, and one cluster per query for MIRACL. This is not article-level clustering, and comparisons are not multiplicity-adjusted. The 95% intervals for 4B-versus-0.6B reranker nDCG gains are above zero for three MLQA and two MIRACL conditions; ACORD crosses zero. Both MIRACL Top-1 intervals cross zero. ACORD candidate Recall@50 is only 22.39% with 15.89% judged coverage for Qwen 4B embeddings; reranking cannot recover relevant clauses outside its pool, so low scores are not solely a reranker issue. No confidence interval was computed for the overall macro average; task intervals cannot be reused or all measurements treated as independent samples.

What latency and size mean

Apple M3 Ultra, 256 GiB unified memory, all-layer Metal offload; Q8_0, llama.cpp build 883 commit 1fd20a7f4, context 8192, one parallel slot and rerank k=50. Total time adds warmed query embedding, exact search and reranking stages, including reused embedding-cache stage times. Loading, indexing, network, queuing and production concurrency are excluded. No independent 30-query ×3 latency repeat study was run. Size is GGUF file size, with no peak RAM/VRAM or other-hardware latency measurement.

How does this compare with published measurements?

Qwen reports cross-task results for multiple embedding and reranker sizes; larger rerankers generally score higher overall. The higher 4B nDCG estimates in all six local conditions follow that direction. This study adds size, latency and task-specific tradeoffs under fixed Q8_0, llama.cpp/Metal and the same Top50 candidates. No clear 0.6B gain on MIRACL Chinese, and different Top-1/nDCG rankings on ACORD, are local observations worth further validation, not universal or first-ever discoveries.

Published evaluations and local observations

Larger rerankers often improve quality: an established trend

Qwen already reports higher English, Chinese and multilingual retrieval averages for its 4B reranker than for 0.6B. Our contribution is a local quality-and-latency check with Qwen 4B retrieval, a fixed top-50 pool and Q8 weights, not a first discovery of scaling benefits.

Qwen3 Embedding official release, 5 June 2025 · Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B official model card

The value of 4B depends on the task and metric

In this MLQA run, upgrading the reranker from 0.6B to 4B adds 5.75 Top1 percentage points for Chinese queries over English documents, versus 1.66 for English over English. ACORD Top1 falls 1.75 points while nDCG rises. Median total latency is about 5.2–5.8 times higher across the six tasks. These are configuration-specific trade-offs, not universal gains.

Qwen3 Embedding technical report, v1

This MIRACL test uses a judged-passage subset, not the full benchmark

Official MIRACL contains 32,893,221 English and 4,934,368 Chinese passages. Here retrieval searches only the union of dev judged passages: 7,921 English and 3,786 Chinese, with 200 queries each. Compare configurations within this subset, not against full-corpus leaderboard scores. Qwen lists MIRACL in supervised training, so this is not evidence of generalization to an unseen dataset.

MIRACL official project repository · Qwen3 Embedding technical report, v1

ACORD needs graded evaluation, with explicit metric differences

Our ACORD Top1 counts grade>0, or at least 2 stars, including same-category clauses that are only helpful; the paper calls 3–5 stars relevant. We use exponential nDCG gains (2^grade−1) and a fixed P@5 denominator of five. The paper uses linear gains and normalizes star precision by the available qualifying clauses. Our values therefore support within-protocol comparisons, not a reproduction of official ACORD scores.
