Why does legal-clause retrieval need closer evaluation?

Legal retrieval is challenging, but published scores are not uniformly low

Local standalone ACORD nDCG ranges from 0.52 to 0.70, numerically below most encyclopedia conditions. Different questions, corpora and judgments prevent attributing the entire gap to the legal domain.

The ACORD paper reports nDCG@10 of 0.540 for BM25 and 0.812 for MiniLM + GPT-4o; the latter still has 5-star P@5 of only 17.2%. This supports difficulty ranking high-quality clauses, not uniformly tiny ACORD scores. Different models, candidate counts and implementations prevent claims of beating or trailing the paper. ACORD · Table D-3

In LegalBench-RAG, text-embedding-3-large with 500-character chunks and no reranking achieves Recall@32 of 18.36% on MAUD and 64.38% on CUAD. OpenContracts reports 18.48% and 64.78%, respectively. Some legal tasks remain difficult across implementations, but these are answer-span recall metrics, not nDCG or generated-answer accuracy. LegalBench-RAG · Table 4 · OpenContracts

Why was a correction needed?

ACORD requires ignoring pairs with unknown judgments. The old code assigned unjudged documents zero gain while retaining rank positions, and used exponential gain. Complete standalone vectors allow full rankings to be recovered: all 399 saved Top50 lists match, and all seven caches pass their original manifest hashes. This corrects evaluation rules; it is not a sudden model improvement. ACORD · C.5 / F.1

Rerankers scored only the old 50 candidates. Removing unjudged documents leaves insufficient candidates and cannot supply missing reranker scores. Comparable corrected reranking requires a new candidate pool and reranking. Diagnostic values below are not corrected official results.
