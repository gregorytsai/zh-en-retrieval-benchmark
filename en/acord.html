ACORD / 28-SETUP RETEST

Legal-clause retrieval: reranking is not a guaranteed improvement.

57 queries, 3,931 clauses and 28 complete retested setups. Compare ranking quality, high-star clauses and waiting time.

How should these results be read?

Unknown qrel pairs are removed, explicit zero grades retained, then Top50 is shared by three rerankers. Linear nDCG uses full-qrels ideal rankings; Top-1 requires at least 3 stars. This controlled judged-top50-v1 protocol is not a production RAG hit rate.

F2LLM 0.6B + Qwen 4B has the highest nDCG estimate at 0.729. A small embedder with 4B reranking can approach leading legal scores, but takes several seconds and is not thereby the default capacity recommendation.

Star P@5 divides by min(5, count of qualifying clauses); 5-star has 29 eligible queries, excluding queries without qualifying clauses rather than zero-filling. It is not legal answer accuracy. Time includes embedding, search, judged filtering and reranking.

Why choose rerankers by task?

Qwen 4B alone scores 0.703; BGE reranking gives 0.675, Qwen 0.6B gives 0.684, and Qwen 4B gives 0.728. On the same candidate pool, a small reranker may move high-quality clauses down; adding models does not guarantee improvement.

With Qwen 0.6B reranking, Qwen 4B and BGE-M3 score 0.684 and 0.682; candidate recall is 95.26% versus 85.30%, yet Top-1 is 66.67% and identical query-by-query. More candidate coverage does not guarantee better final ranking.

Paired analysis and capacity tradeoffs

Legal retrieval is challenging, but published scores are not uniformly low

Local standalone ACORD nDCG ranges from 0.52 to 0.70, numerically below most encyclopedia conditions. Different questions, corpora and judgments prevent attributing the entire gap to the legal domain.

The ACORD paper reports nDCG@10 of 0.540 for BM25 and 0.812 for MiniLM + GPT-4o; the latter still has 5-star P@5 of only 17.2%. This supports difficulty ranking high-quality clauses, not uniformly tiny ACORD scores. Different models, candidate counts and implementations prevent claims of beating or trailing the paper. In LegalBench-RAG, text-embedding-3-large with 500-character chunks and no reranking achieves Recall@32 of 18.36% on MAUD and 64.38% on CUAD. OpenContracts reports 18.48% and 64.78%, respectively. Verification, reproduction and limitations

Full rankings, candidate hashes, every reranker score, query metrics and timing sums were validated. Results cover only 57 queries and this candidate protocol, without significant-winner or service-load claims. Historical tail-ranking discrepancies and Windows test limitations are documented in Methods.
