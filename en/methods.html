Where the numbers come from, and what they mean.

The homepage focuses on model selection. This page explains test conditions, metrics, limits and reproducible evidence.

What this benchmark measures

An embedder turns text into vectors to retrieve similar passages. A reranker scores queries together with candidate passages to reorder them. We evaluate the rank of relevant documents, not generated answers or translation quality.

Configuration codes name the corpus language first and query language second. Thus eng-zho means Chinese-to-English retrieval. English-to-Chinese was not tested.

Fixed conditions across 22 configurations

Seven embedders are tested standalone. Qwen 0.6B/4B/8B, BGE-M3 and EmbeddingGemma 2 are each paired with three rerankers: 22 configurations in total. Across three directions, this gives 66 groups of 100 queries, or 6,600 measurements. F2LLM and Granite have no reranker pairings.

The same 300 direction/query tasks are repeated across model configurations; 6,600 measurements are not 6,600 independent questions. Each direction uses 100 queries selected by SHA-256 with seed 20261022. The full validation corpus is retained, with official qrels defining relevant documents.

Vectors are L2-normalized and searched using exact cosine similarity. The first 50 candidates go to the reranker. BGE-M3 is tested in dense mode only, without sparse or multi-vector hybrid retrieval. All models use Q8_0; quantization precision is not compared.

Metrics in plain language

MetricHow to read it
Top-1The share of tasks with a relevant document ranked first; the main homepage metric.
MRR@10Rewards relevant documents appearing earlier in the first 10 results: rank 1 scores 1, rank 2 scores 1/2.
nDCG@10Measures the positions of relevant documents in the top 10, rewarding earlier ranks. This benchmark uses binary qrels.
Recall@10 / @50The fraction of labeled relevant documents retrieved within the first 10 or 50 results.
Mean / p50 / p95Mean is the arithmetic average; p50 is the median; p95 is the value that 95% of measurements do not exceed. Aggregate p95 values are recomputed from individual measurements, not averaged across groups.

A failure means a qrels-labeled document was not placed in the evaluated position. Qrels may omit other passages that answer the query. Similar content, missing query context and unlabeled relevant passages require human review.

Small differences are not decisive

With 100 queries per direction, one task is one percentage point. One task in the three-direction average is about 0.33 pp. BGE-M3 and F2LLM standalone differ by just one task; prioritize testing your own workload.

The original study reports 45 paired-bootstrap intervals comparing rerankers with the same standalone embedder: 2,000 resamples, fixed seed, n=100 per group. These intervals are exploratory, without multiple-comparison correction. They do not directly establish a significant difference between the 4B and 0.6B rerankers.Download all intervals.

The corpora contain only 430–978 documents, with 100 queries per direction and near-ceiling candidate Recall@50. Results should not be extrapolated directly to long documents, million-document knowledge bases, specialized domains, different quantization or other hardware.

What does this add to published knowledge?

Published knowledge and sourcesObservations in this run
Official Qwen documentation describes multilingual retrieval, task instructions and different model sizes.This run measures direction-specific quality and latency across sizes on fixed GGUF/Metal settings and queries. It does not claim to discover cross-language retrieval.
Official BGE documentation describes retrieving candidates before reranking them.Upgrading Qwen reranking from 0.6B to 4B adds 10.2 pp on average for Chinese-to-English, but only 0.4 pp for English-to-English. The extra compute has different returns by direction.
The Qwen3 paper reports model scale and retrieval/reranking capabilities.All five core embedders with the Qwen 4B reranker score 281/300. The Qwen 0.6B and 8B pairings swap success/failure on two tasks, and their full top-50 rankings differ. Equal totals do not make the models equivalent.
Granite and Google both position their models as compact or efficient embedding options.This site adds weight-size and latency comparisons on the same small corpora. It complements, rather than replaces, official multi-task benchmarks.

These local observations warrant further validation. The web research was not a systematic literature review. No claim of a first discovery or universal effectiveness is made.
